CASE FILE CC-16

The Bag Beneath the Cloth

John Smith withdrew cash from the bank the evening before and left it in a black bag in his car, parked in the porch outside his home. A black cloth covered the bag, making it difficult to notice against the dark seats.

The next afternoon, the cash bag disappeared. John’s car had no broken windows or visible damage. His house camera had been unreliable, and three people had been at or near the house.

Your task: Identify who took the cash bag, where it was taken and how the car was opened.

Investigation rules

This is a fictional case. Exactly one of the three suspects deliberately removed the cash bag, acting alone, between 3.05 and 3.15 p.m. No remote actions, accomplices or unrecorded access routes were involved. All confirmed recordings and timestamps are accurate.

The car stayed in the porch throughout. The bag and cash were confirmed present at 3.05 p.m. and missing at 3.15 p.m. For the grid, assign each suspect a different location at 3.10 p.m. and associated item. Each location and item must be used once.

The case does not endorse leaving cash in a car. Missing fingerprints, ordinary tools and a camera fault do not identify a thief on their own.

The Three Suspects

🛍️

Nora Smith

John’s wife. Wanted a replacement phone because her old screen was cracked. Knew about the money and planned to buy groceries later that afternoon.

🧰

Jeremy Green

A plumber working in the house from 2.40 to 3.20 p.m. Brought a toolbox and was temporarily unsupervised while Nora took a work call.

📦

Tom Frank

A courier who delivered a parcel of hair accessories from 3.00 to 3.03 p.m. Did not ring the doorbell, so Nora did not see him directly.

Wanting a phone, owning tools or delivering hair accessories does not establish theft.

Nora went to the supermarket only from 3.40 to 4.30 p.m., after the confirmed theft interval. That later trip is not her location at 3.10 p.m.

Timeline & Evidence

Exhibit 01 · 3.05 p.m. · Bag confirmed present

Before taking a work call, Nora checked the cash bag in the car. A timestamped inspection recording confirmed the bag and full amount were there. She locked the car, returned the keys to the hall hook and went into the study.

Exhibit 02 · The house camera gap

The house camera lagged from 3.00 to 3.50 p.m. The car’s own camera faced forward and could not show the rear seat. Investigators did not assume that either gap was deliberately caused.

Exhibit 03 · Independent porch coverage

A neighbour’s separate camera continuously covered the only approach to the car from 3.05 to 3.15 p.m. It identified Jeremy as the only person to approach or access the car during that complete interval.

Exhibit 04 · 3.07–3.12 p.m. · The car visit

The independent recording showed Jeremy arriving with his toolbox, opening the car and leaving with a black bag matching the confirmed cash bag. The uninterrupted view showed the bag coming directly from the car, not from his toolbox.

Exhibit 05 · How the door opened

A separate hall recording showed Jeremy taking the car keys at 3.06 p.m. and returning them at 3.13 p.m. The porch view showed him using those keys. Examination confirmed no forced entry or damaged window. No special tool or lock bypass was involved.

Exhibit 06 · Nora’s work call

A verified continuous video call placed Nora in the study from 3.06 to 3.14 p.m. The room view and doorway record confirmed she did not leave. Her grocery bag was beside her, packed for the later shopping trip.

Exhibit 07 · Tom’s delivery record

The courier’s timestamped doorstep photograph and recording confirmed the parcel was delivered at 3.02 p.m. Continuous route footage showed Tom leaving at 3.03 p.m. and making another delivery away from the house at 3.10 p.m. He did not return during the theft interval.

Exhibit 08 · 3.15 p.m. · Bag missing

Nora checked the car immediately after the call and discovered the cash bag missing. The check and the earlier full-bag recording fix the relevant interval. The car remained locked and undamaged.

Exhibit 09 · The object associations

Nora had the grocery bag. Jeremy brought the toolbox. Tom carried the delivery parcel. Each suspect is associated with one different listed item in the grid.

Exhibit 10 · Fingerprints

Only John’s usable fingerprints were recovered near the door handle. This does not establish that nobody else touched it or who might have worn gloves. The independent recordings, rather than missing prints, establish the theft.

Exhibit 11 · The toolbox and hair accessories

A toolbox can be normal equipment for a plumber, and hair accessories can be an ordinary delivery. Investigators found no reason to infer that either was used to bypass the car lock.

Exhibit 12 · The camera wires

There was no confirmed evidence identifying who caused the house-camera fault. Any contact with wiring would need its own explanation and timing. The theft is solvable without claiming Jeremy disabled the camera.

Teacher Case Solution

Jeremy Green · Car porch · John’s car keys

The cash bag was present at 3.05 p.m. and missing at 3.15 p.m. Independent porch coverage identified Jeremy as the sole visitor to the car, and showed him removing the same bag directly from it. The hall and porch recordings establish use of John’s keys.

Nora remained in the study during the work call. Tom had left before the theft window and was recorded on his delivery route at 3.10 p.m. Nora’s later grocery trip does not contradict her earlier study location.

The camera fault, lack of fingerprints, plumber’s tools and hair accessories are not the decisive evidence. The case does not establish who caused the camera fault and does not require a vehicle-entry technique.

Ending: investigators recover the bag and refer the verified evidence to the appropriate authorities. John moves the recovered cash to secure storage. Recovery is an ending, not an extra clue needed to identify the thief.
